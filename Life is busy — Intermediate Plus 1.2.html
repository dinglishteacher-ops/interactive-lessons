Part two

Busy-ness is not a competition

Текст на тему радіопрограми BBC — з питаннями й дискусією.

6
Reading

Why does everybody claim to be busy?

Read the article. What problem does it focus on?

Busy-ness is NOT a competition

In the modern workplace, when a colleague asks how you are, the answer is almost always the same: "Busy. So busy." It sounds like a complaint — but are we secretly proud of it?

Notice what happens when people come back from holiday. Twenty years ago, the ritual was simple: you returned with photographs, you talked about the food and the weather, and everyone was slightly jealous. Today the first thing most people say is how many emails were waiting for them and how little of the holiday they actually managed to switch off. The story is no longer about the holiday. It is about the backlog.

Something strange has happened to the way we signal status. For most of history, the people at the top demonstrated their position through leisure: the whole point of being wealthy was that you did not have to rush anywhere. Now the opposite is true. Being permanently in demand has become the brand. Busy means needed, needed means important, and important means successful — so we perform our busy-ness for each other, and social media makes it very easy to show what we are doing at seven in the morning and eleven at night.

The trouble is that this quietly changes how we judge people. If you are not visibly busy, you risk being seen as someone who is not delivering, or worse, someone nobody wants to work with. So we compare sixty-hour weeks the way people used to compare cars, and "busy" becomes equated with progress.

And yet almost nobody says they enjoy it. We have more time-saving technology than any generation before us and we somehow feel we have less time than ever. Perhaps the real question is not how we can fit more in, but why we are so addicted to the feeling of being needed in the first place.

7
Reading · comprehension

Answer the questions

Answer in full sentences first, then check the model answer.

  1. How has the post-holiday ritual changed in recent years? What happened before, and what happens now?
    People used to come back and talk about the holiday itself — the photos, the food, the weather. Now they talk about how many emails were waiting for them and how hard it was to switch off, so the conversation is about the backlog rather than the break.
  2. According to the article, why do we all claim to be so busy?
    Because being busy now signals status. Busy means you are in demand, being needed means you are important, and being important means you are successful — so people perform their busy-ness for each other.
  3. How did rich people show their status in the past, and why is that interesting?
    They showed it through leisure — the point of being wealthy was not having to rush. It's interesting because the signal has completely reversed: today we compete over how little free time we have.

Part three

Present simple vs continuous

Грамматика юніту + вимова: як слова зчіплюються у мовленні.

10
Grammar · reference

Two presents, two jobs

Present simple

I work ten hours a day.

Постійне, регулярне, завжди так. Розклади, факти, звички, стани.

Present continuous

I'm working ten hours a day.

Тимчасове, зараз або в цей період життя. Також зміни й тенденції.

Reference
  • Маркери simple: always, usually, every day, on Mondays, twice a week, never. Маркери continuous: at the moment, right now, today, this week, these days, currently.
  • State verbs зазвичай не вживаються в continuous: know, believe, understand, need, want, like, love, hate, prefer, mean, belong, own, seem, agree. → I need a break, не I'm needing.
  • Але деякі змінюють значення: I think it's true (думка) / I'm thinking about it (процес); I have a car (володіння) / I'm having lunch (дія); It tastes good / I'm tasting the soup.
  • always + -ing = роздратування або здивування: He's always taking on too much! — не факт, а емоція.
  • Тенденції та зміни — тільки continuous: People are working longer hours than they used to.

Pronunciation · connected speech

Where do the words link?

In natural speech a final consonant joins the next word when it begins with a vowel sound: take_on sounds like tay-kon. Tap every join where linking happens — some sentences have more than one. Then say each sentence smoothly, without pauses.

  1. I'lltakeiton.
    Два з'єднання: take‿it та it‿on — звучить як «tay-ki-ton». Між I'll і take лінкінгу немає: далі йде приголосний /t/.
  2. Shemakestimeforus.
    Лінкується тільки for‿us — «fo-rus». Після makes і time йдуть приголосні, тож там з'єднання немає.
  3. Keepupwiththem.
    keep‿up = «kee-pup». А with them — це не лінкінг, а злиття двох /ð/-звуків в один.
  4. Ifeeloverwhelmed.
    feel‿overwhelmed = «fee-lover-whelmed». Між I та feel лінкінгу немає — там голосний перед приголосним.
