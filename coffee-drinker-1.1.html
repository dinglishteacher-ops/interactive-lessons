Ten things you probably didn't know about coffee

Read the text aloud. What information is new to you?Сірим — транскрипція складних слів і назв: читай уголос і став наголос там, де стоїть ˈ. Підсвічені фрази — це граматика другої частини.

The world's favourite drug

For millions of us coffee is a big part of our lifestyle. Early birds crave it, people who work long hours need it, and those with active lifestyles drink it for energy. But how much do we actually know about it?

  1. Coffee was discovered in Ethiopia /ˌiːθiˈəʊpiə/ in the ninth century AD. The legend goes that a goat-herder called Khalid /ˈkɑːlɪd/ noticed his goats becoming lively after eating strange berries in the mountains. He carried some of the beans down to his village and had them turned into a drink.
  2. The drink only became popular after it reached the port of Mocha /ˈmɒkə/ in Yemen /ˈjemən/ — and the port gave the drink its name. From Mocha the beans were exported to India, Java /ˈdʒɑːvə/ and eventually to Europe.
  3. Another version of the story says beans were not allowed to leave Yemen, until a seventeenth-century traveller named Baba Budan /ˈbɑːbə buˈdɑːn/ hid seven of them and took them to India. There he had them planted and harvested by local farmers. Every coffee plant in the world today is said to come from those seven beans.
  4. The name comes from the Arabic qahwat al-bun /ˌkɑːhwɑːt æl ˈbʊn/, "wine of the bean". It was shortened to qahwa /ˈkɑːhwə/, which turned into the Turkish kahve /ˈkɑːhve/, and finally into coffee.
  5. The world's first coffee house was opened in 1475 in Constantinople /ˌkɒnstæntɪˈnəʊpl/ — the city we now call Istanbul /ˌɪstænˈbʊl/.
  6. Two kinds of coffee are grown commercially: arabica /əˈræbɪkə/ and robusta /rəʊˈbʌstə/. Arabica tastes smoother and is used in most speciality coffees; robusta is cheaper, contains more caffeine /ˈkæfiːn/ and survives in tougher conditions.
  7. The two oldest cats ever recorded were given coffee every single morning. Creme Puff, who reached the age of thirty-eight, was fed coffee, bacon, eggs and broccoli /ˈbrɒkəli/ by her owner, Jake Perry — who also owned the previous record holder, Grandpa Rex Allen, thirty-four, on exactly the same diet.
  8. After tea, coffee is the most popular drink on the planet: around 500 billion cups a year. It is also the second most traded commodity /kəˈmɒdəti/ after oil. Scandinavians /ˌskændɪˈneɪviənz/ drink the most — in Finland /ˈfɪnlənd/, Norway /ˈnɔːweɪ/ and Sweden /ˈswiːdn/ the average is two cups per person per day.
  9. In the seventeenth century a French doctor told his patients to add milk to their coffee. Coffee had never been drunk with milk before, and that is where café au lait /ˌkæfeɪ əʊ ˈleɪ/ comes from. More recently, coffee has been shown to have several health benefits: it can work as an anti-depressant /ˌæntidɪˈpresnt/, help the body burn fat and may protect people from Alzheimer's /ˈæltshaɪməz/, Parkinson's /ˈpɑːkɪnsnz/ and dementia /dɪˈmenʃə/.
  10. A third of the world's coffee is produced in Brazil /brəˈzɪl/, the leading producer, followed by Vietnam /ˌviːetˈnæm/, Colombia /kəˈlʌmbiə/ and Indonesia /ˌɪndəˈniːʒə/.

Grammar · discovery

Work out the rules

Look at these sentences from the text, then choose the correct alternative.Спершу подивись на речення, потім обери правильний варіант правила.

  1. Coffee was discovered in Ethiopia.
  2. Coffee has been shown to have health benefits.
  3. Coffee had never before been drunk with milk.
  4. Budan had the beans planted and harvested by local farmers.
Three sentences don't say who did the action. Which one is the exception?
  1. We use the passive to emphasise
  2. The person who does the action is unimportant, obvious or
  3. We form the passive with subject + to be +
Reference · the passive
  • Present simple: is / are + V3 — Coffee is grown in Brazil.
  • Past simple: was / were + V3 — It was discovered in Ethiopia.
  • Present perfect: has / have been + V3 — It has been shown to help.
  • Past perfect: had been + V3 — It had never been drunk with milk.
  • Continuous: is / was being + V3 — My car is being repaired.
  • Modals & future: will / must / can + be + V3 — The beans must be kept dry.
  • The doer, if we need it: by + person — She was fed by her owner.

Grammar · causative have

Arranged, or a bad experience?

Reference · causative have
  • Form: have + object + past participle — I had my hair cut.
  • We use it when we arrange for somebody else to do something for us: We're having our roof fixed.
  • We also use it for a bad experience or an action by someone unknown: He had his wallet stolen.
  • Compare: I cut my hair (myself) vs I had my hair cut (at the hairdresser's).

Decide what each sentence means.

  1. We had our coffee machine repaired.
  2. He had his wallet stolen on the metro.
  3. She's having her kitchen painted this week.
  4. They had their windows broken by the storm.
  5. I had my nails done before the wedding.
